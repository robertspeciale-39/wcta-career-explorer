How it works

Four short parts, one clear picture.

There are no right or wrong answers. Go with your first instinct and answer for who you are today.

1. Interests

Rate 60 activities from "strongly dislike" to "strongly like." This shows which of six interest types describes you best.

2. Skills

Rate your confidence in 12 skill areas, then try 12 quick skill-check questions. We compare how you see yourself with how you did.

3. Work values

Tell us what matters most in a job, like independence, helping others, recognition, or a steady paycheck.

4. Your results

See charts of your interests, strengths, and growth areas, your top five careers, and colleges to explore, starting with UNLV, UNR, and CSN.

Methodology

Built on how career counselors actually do it.

This quiz follows the same basic recipe as well-known career tools: measure interests, skills, and values, then compare them to occupations. Here is what we based it on.

Interests: Holland's six types

Psychologist John Holland described six interest types: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, and Conventional (RIASEC). The quiz uses 60 statements, 10 for each type. That is the same layout as the O*NET Interest Profiler Short Form, which reported scale reliabilities of .78 to .87. Our statements are original, written for this mock-up.

Skills: self-ratings plus a quick check

The ASVAB Career Exploration Program reports verbal, math, and science and technical skills. We use 12 self-rated skills plus 12 short, untimed questions covering verbal, number, spatial, and mechanical reasoning. This is a screener, not a validated test.

Work values: six categories

We borrow the six work values used in the ASVAB program: achievement, working conditions, recognition, relationships, support, and independence. Careers are matched to the two values that fit them best.

Matching: interests count most

Each career gets a fit score: 60% interests, 25% skills, and 15% values. Interests are weighted most because research links interest fit to job performance and staying on a path. Your scores are compared to your own average, so liking everything (or nothing) does not skew the list.

What this quiz is, and is not

Research finds that matching your interests to a field has a moderate link to success and persistence, and the effect varies by field and by person (Nye et al., 2012; Ertl et al., 2022). So treat your results as a starting point for exploring, not a prediction or a rule. Talk with a counselor, job shadow, and keep an open mind.

Your answers stay with you

This version does not save your answers or send them anywhere. They live in your browser tab until you close it. At the end you can try the email option, but in this mock-up it does not actually send.
